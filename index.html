Deathtouch ▸

Deathtouch

A creature with deathtouch deals lethal damage with any amount of damage, even 1.

  • Any nonzero amount of combat damage assigned to a creature by a source with deathtouch is considered lethal damage.
  • Deathtouch doesn't trigger on non-combat damage unless the source explicitly says so—but the rules actually apply to any damage from a source with deathtouch.
  • Multiple instances of deathtouch are redundant.
Flying ▸

Flying

A creature with flying can't be blocked except by creatures with flying or reach.

  • Flying is an evasion ability that restricts blocking.
  • Creatures without flying or reach cannot block a creature with flying.
  • Multiple instances of flying are redundant.
Reach ▸

Reach

A creature with reach can block creatures with flying.

  • Reach allows a creature to block attackers with flying that it otherwise couldn't.
  • Reach does not grant flying—the creature still can't be used to satisfy "flying" requirements.
  • Multiple instances of reach are redundant.
First Strike ▸

First Strike

A creature with first strike deals combat damage before creatures without first strike.

  • Combat damage is dealt in two steps: first strikers deal damage, then everyone else does.
  • If a first striker kills its blocker, the blocker deals no combat damage back.
  • Granting first strike after the first strike damage step doesn't retroactively deal damage.
Double Strike ▸

Double Strike

A creature with double strike deals combat damage in both the first strike and normal combat damage steps.

  • The creature effectively hits twice—once during first strike damage and once during regular damage.
  • Double strike includes first strike; the creature does not get a third hit.
  • If blocked by a creature that dies in the first strike step, the double striker still deals its second instance of damage to the player (if it has trample).
Indestructible ▸

Indestructible

A permanent with indestructible can't be destroyed.

  • Effects that say "destroy" don't affect indestructible permanents.
  • Lethal damage and damage from a source with deathtouch don't destroy indestructible creatures, but the damage stays marked on them.
  • Sacrificing or exiling an indestructible permanent still works—those aren't destruction.
Hexproof ▸

Hexproof

A permanent with hexproof can't be the target of spells or abilities your opponents control.

  • Only opponent spells and abilities are affected—you can still target your own hexproof permanents.
  • Non-targeting effects (like board wipes) still affect hexproof creatures.
  • Aura spells on the stack don't target, so an aura entering the battlefield attached to a hexproof creature works if it wasn't cast targeting it.
Hexproof (White) ▸

Hexproof from White

This permanent can't be the target of white spells or abilities your opponents control, or of white sources your opponents control.

  • Protection is color-specific—spells of other colors can still target it.
  • Prevents all damage from white sources, not just targeting.
  • White creatures can't block this permanent.

Poison ▸

Poison Counters

A player who has ten or more poison counters loses the game.

  • Poison counters are given to players, not creatures.
  • Creatures with toxic/toxic X deal X poison counters in addition to normal combat damage.
  • Poison counters persist—there's no natural way to remove them outside of specific card effects.
  • Infect damage gives poison counters equal to the damage dealt instead of regular damage.
Trample ▸

Trample

Excess combat damage dealt by a creature with trample to a blocking creature carries over to the player or planeswalker it's attacking.

  • You must assign at least lethal damage to all blockers before assigning leftover damage to the player/planeswalker.
  • If no creatures block, all damage goes to the player/planeswalker.
  • Trample works with deathtouch—any nonzero damage is considered lethal, so the rest tramples over.
Haste ▸

Haste

A creature with haste can attack and tap on the turn it enters the battlefield.

  • Normally, creatures can't attack or use tap abilities the turn they come under your control ("summoning sickness").
  • Haste only affects the "can't attack/tap" restriction—it doesn't prevent other restrictions.
  • Losing haste after attacking doesn't remove the creature from combat.
Menace ▸

Menace

A creature with menace can't be blocked except by two or more creatures.

  • One blocker is not enough—at least two creatures must block it.
  • If one of the two blockers is removed before damage, the block is still legal (the creature remains blocked).
  • Multiple instances of menace are redundant—still just needs two blockers.
Summoning Sickness ▸

Summoning Sickness

A creature can't attack or use tap abilities the turn it comes under your control, unless it has haste.

  • Summoning sickness is not a keyword ability—it's a game rule that applies to all creatures.
  • It only affects attacking and abilities with the tap symbol—untap abilities and non-tap activated abilities work fine.
  • A creature that has been under your control since the beginning of your most recent turn is no longer "sick."
  • Gaining control of an opponent's creature gives it summoning sickness (unless it has haste).
Goad ▸

Goad

A goaded creature attacks each combat if able, and attacks a player other than the goader if able.

  • Goaded creatures must attack—they can't choose to stay back.
  • If the goaded creature can't attack the goading player (e.g., due to a restriction), it attacks another player or a planeswalker.
  • If goaded by multiple players, the creature avoids all of them if able.
  • Goad lasts until your next turn.
Defender ▸

Defender

A creature with defender can't attack.

  • Defender doesn't prevent the creature from blocking—it's purely an attack restriction.
  • Creatures with defender can still use abilities that cost tapping.
  • If a creature loses defender (via an effect), it can attack normally.
Vigilance ▸

Vigilance

Attacking doesn't cause a creature with vigilance to tap.

  • The creature still declares an attack as normal—it just doesn't tap when doing so.
  • This means it can both attack and block on subsequent turns without needing to untap.
  • Vigilance only applies to attacking, not to other tap-based abilities.
Lore Counter ▸

Lore Counters

Sagas are enchantments that gain a lore counter at the beginning of your end step, triggering chapter abilities.

  • Chapter abilities trigger based on the number of lore counters—Chapter I triggers on the first, Chapter II on the second, etc.
  • Once the final chapter resolves, the Saga is sacrificed as a state-based action.
  • Lore counters can be added or removed by other card effects to speed up or reset Sagas.
Copy ▸

Copy

Copies replicate an existing permanent or spell exactly—including its characteristics and abilities.

  • Copies don't copy the original's counters, attachments, or temporary effects unless stated.
  • If copying a creature, the copy is not a token unless the effect says so—it enters as a duplicate.
  • Most copies are tokens created by the copy effect itself.
  • If you copy a permanent that's a copy of something else, you copy the current state of the first copy.
Can't Attack ▸

Can't Attack

The creature is unable to declare an attack.

  • This can be caused by abilities like defender, or by external effects that prohibit attacking.
  • If the restriction is removed (e.g., defender is stripped), the creature can attack normally.
  • Effects that force a creature to attack won't override a "can't attack" restriction—restrictions win over requirements.
Can't Block ▸

Can't Block

The creature is unable to declare a block.

  • This is typically imposed by another creature's ability (e.g., "creatures your opponents control can't block").
  • If the effect ends before the declare blockers step, the creature can block normally.
  • Like with attacking, "can't block" restrictions override effects that would force a creature to block.
Shield Counter ▸

Shield Counter

If a permanent with a shield counter would be destroyed or deal damage to a player, remove a shield counter instead.

  • A shield counter prevents damage from being dealt to a player and protects the permanent from being destroyed once.
  • The shield counter is removed when either of these effects happen.
  • Multiple shield counters protect the permanent multiple times.
  • Shield counters are physical counters placed on the permanent—they can be added or removed by various card effects.
  • This differs from Ward—Ward counters an opponent's spell/ability targeting the permanent, while Shield protects from damage/destruction effects.
Finality ▸

Finality

If a permanent with finality would be put into a graveyard from the battlefield, exile it instead.

  • This is a replacement effect, not a trigger—it modifies where the permanent goes.
  • Finality prevents reanimation strategies—the card can't be brought back from the graveyard because it never gets there.
  • If the permanent is token, it ceases to exist instead (tokens can't be exiled as objects, they just vanish).
Foretell ▸

Foretell

While you have priority, you may pay {2} and exile a card from your hand face down. You may cast it later for its foretell cost.

  • When you foretell a card, you exile it face down as a separate game object—you retain knowledge of what it is.
  • On a later turn, you can cast the foretold card from exile by paying its foretell cost instead of its normal mana cost.
  • The foretell cost is typically lower than the card's normal mana cost, allowing you to play cards ahead of tempo.
  • If a card has multiple foretell values or conditions, you follow whichever is printed on the card.
  • You can foretell multiple cards during the same turn, but only one card may be foretold at a time by most cards.
  • Forgetting about a foretold card (e.g., losing track of which turn it was foretold) doesn't prevent you from casting it later.
